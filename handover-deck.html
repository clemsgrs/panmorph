01Project handover · 27 Aug 2026

PanMorph

Mapping cross-organ transfer in histology-based genomic biomarker prediction.

Phase 1 · Strong passPhase 2 · Designed, not built

We predict MSI status from H&E slides with frozen PRISM embeddings.
We ask: does a model trained on one organ work on another organ?
Phase 1 answered: yes — the transfer is real, confound-free, and organ-dependent.

02Motivation

The obvious claim was a trap

The project began with a hope: multi-organ training improves biomarker prediction. An early pilot seemed to agree. Prostate TP53 AUC rose from 0.72 to 0.75 with foreign organs.

But the enriched run also grew training positives from ~24 to ~549. The gain had two mundane explanations before any biological one.

Confound A — sample size

More positives help any model. Organ identity may be irrelevant.

Confound B — site shortcut

TCGA slides carry hospital signatures. Models can cheat on them (Howard et al. 2021).

So we reframed the project. The question is not whether multi-organ training helps. The question is when transfer is positive, negative, or absent — and why.

04Experiments

One pre-registered gate

Phase 1 was scoped as a go/no-go decision, not a paper. The null to kill: off-diagonal AUC is explained by sample size or site signatures, not shared morphology.

1 · Honest ceilings

Within-organ AUC with leave-site-out CV. Random CV is reported only as the site-inflated contrast.

2 · Zero-shot matrix

Train on source organ(s). Test on a never-seen target. Patient-level bootstrap CIs.

3 · Permutation null

Shuffle source labels 1000×. Empirical p per matrix cell.

Pass rule (fixed in advance)

CI lower bound > 0.60 and perm p < 0.05, in both COAD↔STAD directions.

The model is deliberately boring: a fixed logistic probe, no tuning. A separate diagnostic showed the threat was real — TCGA site is decodable from the embeddings (balanced accuracy 0.58–0.73 vs chance ≈0.1). The gate had to beat that shortcut.

06Interpretation

Real, but organ-dependent

  • GI↔GI transfer is real and confound-free. COAD and STAD are both Lynch/dMMR GI cancers. Zero shared sites; the target is never trained on.
  • UCEC is the odd one out. It barely transfers in any direction (p ≈ 0.10–0.44), yet it is individually predictable (0.755). This is the atlas thesis, not a failure.
  • We beat the closest prior work. Lee et al. 2025 (same organs, same labels, tile CNNs) got ~0.57 for CRC→STAD — chance level. Our frozen-PRISM probe gets 0.760. Both studies agree UCEC stands apart.
  • Cite the full run. The quick run (100 perms) misread COAD→UCEC as significant. At 1000 perms it is not (p = .099).

08Future work

Three research questions, one queue

Agreed scope: MSI first, as a complete story. TP53 follows as the second biomarker.

RQ1 · Value primary · next

How many local positives is a foreign organ worth?

Experiment E1: warm-start vs cold-start over k added target positives. Confirmatory rung pre-registered: COAD→STAD, pooled base, k=10. Fully specified in the PRD (34 user stories). Ready to build.

RQ2 · Capacity secondary

Does the transfer survive a stronger head?

Ladder: linear → MLP (unblocked) → MIL (blocked on tile features). If MIL cracks UCEC, the GI↔endometrium wall was an aggregation artifact, not biology.

RQ3 · Mechanism blocked

What governs whether transfer happens?

One transferring pair = n≈1 of variation. Honest answer: parked until we add MSI-labeled organs. This motivates cohort expansion.
