…but their cross-organ transfer stalls — and a confound is left open

Cross-tissue · near chance

CRC→STAD ≈ 0.57, GYN↔GI ≈ 0.57. Best pair STAD→CRC ≈ 0.72 (asymmetric). Multi-tissue training is a wash — equal/worse for CRC & STAD, helps only the weak UCEC model.

→ No clean cross-organ generalization.

Confound · unaddressed

They cite Howard 2021 on TCGA site signatures — then never control for it. No site-disjoint design, no permutation null. So "transfer vs. site vs. more data" stays entangled.

→ Can't tell real transfer from artifact.

Convergent on one point: UCEC is the odd organ out — which our own result will echo.

Two gaps we set out to close

Representation

Pathology foundation model + MIL

Frozen PRISM slide embeddings now (baseline), tile encoder + MIL next — not ImageNet tile-classifiers averaged. We have slide-level labels, not per-tile ones, so a per-tile classifier is the wrong tool.

Confound-clean design

Make transfer un-fakeable

Test on an unseen organ, across cohorts that share zero TCGA sites, with a label-permutation null. Neither sample size nor a site shortcut can pass.

That second gap is the whole game — so first, how easy is it to be fooled?

More data alone could explain the gain. To claim cross-organ transfer, we must rule out two confounds.

Two ways to be fooled

Confound 1 · sample size

More examples, not more biology

Any classifier improves with more training data. Adding organs adds data — the gain may have nothing to do with shared morphology.

Confound 2 · site shortcut

The embedding encodes the scanner

PRISM embeddings predict the tissue-source-site far above chance (STAD 0.73, COAD 0.61, UCEC 0.58; chance ≈ 0.1). If site correlates with MSI, the model can cheat.

The original enrichment result can't separate either confound from real transfer. So we retire it as the headline and design a clean test.

The clean test — zero-shot, and the sites don't overlap

Kills confound 1 · by construction

Train on one organ, test on another

Train on stomach. Test on colon — never shown to the model. The target contributes zero training cases, so "more data" cannot explain any signal.

Kills confound 2 · by construction

COAD, UCEC, STAD share zero sites

The three cohorts come from entirely disjoint TCGA centers (24 / 29 / 22 sites, 0 shared). A site shortcut learned on the source has nothing to fire on in the target.

Plus a label-permutation null (shuffle source labels, refit) as the significance test — so a pipeline artifact can't pass either.

Cross-organ MSI transfer is real — but organ-specific

GI ↔ GI · real transfer

COAD ↔ STAD both directions: AUC 0.74–0.76, perm p < 0.005. Stomach-trained → colon (0.74) nearly matches colon's own honest ceiling (0.77), with the target unseen and sites disjoint.

→ Not sample size. Not site. Shared morphology.

GI ↔ endometrium · no transfer

UCEC doesn't transfer in any direction (p = 0.10–0.44), despite being predictable on its own (0.755). MSI morphology does not carry between GI and endometrium for a linear probe.

→ This is the finding, not a failure.

Transfer is real where the biology is shared (Lynch/dMMR GI cancers) and absent where it isn't. That asymmetry is the science.

The gate is passed — what we build next

Phase 2 · value primary

E1 — how many local positives is a foreign organ worth? Subsample a label-rich target down a k-sweep against its real ceiling; the low-k lift is the substitution rate. Lowest rungs (≈2–3 positives) are the genuinely-scarce regime — measured, not demonstrated. Budget-matched swap is a secondary dual control.

Phase 2 · method

Capacity ladder (MLP head → trainable MIL, encoder frozen) is the primary representation probe. A shared-subspace probe runs only if the pooled base shows negative transfer — demoted to contingent (underpowered at ~2 sharing organs).

Phase 2 · scope

TP53 atlas (PRAD/BRCA/BLCA) — a more organ-specific biomarker, and the route to powering the parked mechanism question. Blocked on BRCA+BLCA PRISM features.
